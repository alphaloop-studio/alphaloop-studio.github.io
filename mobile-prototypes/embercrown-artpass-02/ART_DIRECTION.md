# EMBERCROWN Art Direction — The Cinder Oath

## One-sentence identity

A hand-built isometric dark fantasy where broken crown shards orbit those who inherit an oath, and every warm light represents a promise deliberately restored.

## What we reject

- Photorealism without the budget to support it
- Huge empty maps
- Free cameras that expose unfinished scenery
- Excessive bloom, particles, screen shake, and UI noise
- Generic purple corruption as the only sign of evil
- Feature breadth used to disguise weak character presence

## What we amplify

### Tabletop diorama composition
Orthographic projection, compact sightlines, readable silhouettes, and visible level edges make the limitation intentional.

### The material quartet
- Ash: loss and abandoned names
- Brass: duty and imperfect permanence
- Moss: time reclaiming certainty
- Ember: promises that require active maintenance

### Crown shards as narrative anatomy
The player carries three warm shards. The boss carries three distorted void shards. They reveal how each character relates to inheritance and duty.

### Restrained combat language
- Sword: warm gold arc
- Ember spell: orange core and clear travel path
- Dodge: pale blue ring
- Enemy attack: thin ground telegraph
- Boss corruption: violet reserved for the oathbreaker

## Acceptance criteria

1. The player remains recognisable at phone scale without reading the HUD.
2. Player and boss differ by silhouette and crown-shard colour.
3. Each combat action has one dominant colour and one readable timing cue.
4. No effect obscures the player for more than 150 ms.
5. Remote GLB failure never prevents game start.
6. Mobile landscape is the reference composition; portrait remains playable rather than blocked.

## Next gate

Do not add inventory or regions until physical-device checks cover start reliability, simultaneous move and attack, 15-minute thermal stability, boss frame pacing, GLB animation mapping, and throttled-network fallback.
